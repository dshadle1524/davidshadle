## Notes

### What changed in draft 2

| Was | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "five executive reporting surfaces" | "three executive reporting products" | Factually wrong. There are three: PM Workshop, Portfolio Operations, Technical Due Diligence. |
| "owns the experience layer" | "advised on and implemented the experience layer" | "Owned" overstated it. This is the accurate verb pair. |
| Rulebook described in mechanical detail | Two or three sentences, high level | Describe it, do not sell it. The claim is what it lets you deliver. |
| Rulebook as "my method" | "I work from a rulebook" | Removes the implication that you invented or own it. |
| Method absent from the 300 | Problem-brief-first added | It is the more distinctive habit and it needs no caveat. |

**Draft 4 correction.** The database rebuild was the Portfolio Operations dashboard, not the workshop dashboards. Corrected everywhere.

**Draft 3 change.** The agent framing was rewritten across the 300 and LinkedIn. Agents are now described by how they are used inside strategy work, to identify problems and opportunities and to build alignment before execution. The earlier version described building agents as an activity in itself, which put the emphasis on the tooling rather than on the better strategy work it produces.

### The pressure test

Every claim was checked against one question: if someone asked a single follow up, would it hold?

Two things were cut for failing it. The three products are no longer described as sharing a common foundation, because that invites a question about what exactly is shared. The rulebook is no longer described mechanically, because detail invites scrutiny of a method rather than attention to the delivery outcome.

The remaining claims are narrow enough to be boring under questioning, which is the correct target.

### Deliberately absent

- Tenure. February 2026 is on the resume where a reader can see it.
- Availability. Evidence of current work does that job better.
- Client names. Both engagements described by category.
- The AI coach and PM toolkit. Advisory work, and there is stronger material competing for the space.
- Any speaker framing.

### Two judgment calls for your review

1. **"Full stack product practitioner" appears only in the 300.** It needs surrounding evidence to earn it. In the 50 it would read as assertion.
2. **The AI transition paragraph appears only on LinkedIn.** That is where people are working out what AI means for their own careers, so it lands. In third person it reads as explaining yourself.

### One thing I want your read on

The 300 now carries two method ideas: problem first, then rulebook. That is the ceiling. A third would tip it from describing how you work into teaching a methodology, which is the line you drew. If it already feels close to that line, the rulebook sentence is the one to cut, since problem-first is more distinctive and needs no defending.
