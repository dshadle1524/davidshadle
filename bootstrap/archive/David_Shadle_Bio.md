# David Shadle — Bio

Five versions. Same substance, same voice: short sentences, active, no em-dashes, no filler.

The role is defined first: leadership, product strategy, user experience, AI-engineering. The projects follow as evidence.

**Attribution:** PM Portal is designed and built end to end, sole responsibility. Workshop redesign is co-led with the program team. Dashboards are defined and directed. AI agents are advisory.

**Voice split:** the website, speaker, and LinkedIn versions keep "close-in work." The resume and internal directory versions drop it, because a skimming reader shouldn't have to decode an idiom.

---

## 1. Personal website / speaker bio
*~290 words. Keeps the idiom.*

David Shadle is a Technical Advisor at Banyan Software, working across a portfolio of more than 100 B2B SaaS operating companies. The role sits at the intersection of leadership, product strategy, user experience, and AI-engineering. It is close-in work. Get inside how a business actually operates, identify the opportunity that matters, and define what success looks like before anyone builds anything.

Strategy is where most of it starts. With his program team, he co-led the redesign of Banyan's AI PM Workshop from a training curriculum into an execution engagement. Companies no longer complete a course. They finish with four artifacts they own: a CEO-signed product strategy pressure-tested against AI disruption, a RICE-scored and capacity-validated roadmap, a customer-facing roadmap ready for any QBR, and an initiative ledger that traces every decision back to evidence.

He designed and built the PM Portal end to end, the rulebook-driven platform the program runs on, and advised on the AI agents that support it, including an on-demand PM coach and a 53-skill product management toolkit. He also defines and directs Banyan's dashboard work, scoping the problem, writing the brief, setting the architecture, and directing the build for the dashboards behind workshop delivery, executive readouts, portfolio cloud spend, technology due diligence, and time reporting. Each serves a different executive audience from a common foundation.

His engineering approach is rulebook-driven. Declare a domain once in a model that is both machine-readable and readable by the person who owns the business meaning, then derive the database, API, documentation, tests, and interface from it. Consistency comes from derivation rather than discipline.

He works between executives, operating partners, and product teams. He frames the problem before the solution, puts open questions in the room rather than hiding them, and hands back work the team owns.

## Notes

- Confirm the portfolio count you want to standardize on. 111 appears in the FinOps work and 107 in the portal work. "100+" is safe everywhere.
- No prior-career or education line is included. If you want one, it slots cleanly as a final sentence in versions 1 and 2.
- "Consistency comes from derivation rather than discipline" is your line and the most quotable thing in the record. It appears in versions 1 through 3 on purpose.
- Calling the portal "rulebook-driven" in the build sentence sets up the engineering paragraph that follows, so the two read as one argument rather than two facts.
